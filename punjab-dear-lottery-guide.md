# Punjab Dear Lottery Guide: Schemes, Timings, Ticket Prices and How to Check Results

Punjab runs one of India's most structured state lottery families. Under the Punjab Dear banner, there is a daily draw for regular players, monthly draws for bigger jackpots, a quarterly draw for the largest routine prize, and seasonal bumper draws that arrive around festivals. If you hold a Punjab ticket — or you are thinking of buying one — this guide explains every scheme, what it costs, when it is drawn, how the prizes work, and how to check your number safely.

## Punjab Dear 50: the daily draw

Dear 50 is Punjab's everyday lottery. It is drawn daily at 6:30 PM in Ludhiana, and each ticket costs Rs 50. Every day of the week has its own draw name: Sunday is Ranger, Monday is Beast, Tuesday is Bronco, Wednesday is Buster, Thursday is Chief, Friday is Colt, and Saturday is Jackal. The day name matters because it tells you exactly which draw your ticket belongs to — always match the day name and the date on your ticket with the result you are checking.

The first prize in Dear 50 is Rs 21 lakh. There is also a consolation prize of Rs 10,000. Lower prize tiers are paid on matching the last four digits of the winning numbers, so even a ticket that does not win the first prize can still win a smaller amount. Because the ticket costs only Rs 50, Dear 50 is the scheme most Punjab players follow day to day, and it is the one whose results appear most often in daily result listings.

## Punjab Dear 100: the monthly draw

Dear 100 is a monthly draw, held on the second Tuesday of the month, and each ticket costs Rs 100. The first prize is Rs 25 lakh and the second prize is Rs 5 lakh. Below that, there are full-number prizes in the third tier, and further tiers paid on matching the last four digits. The step up from Rs 50 to Rs 100 buys a larger first prize and a different prize ladder, which is why many players who play Dear 50 daily also buy a Dear 100 ticket once a month.

A practical tip: because Dear 100 runs only once a month, it is easy to confuse it with a daily draw. Before you check, confirm the scheme name on your ticket — "Dear 100" — and the draw date. Checking a Dear 100 ticket against a Dear 50 result, or against the wrong month's draw, is one of the most common reasons players wrongly think they have lost.

## Punjab Dear 200: the bigger monthly draw

Dear 200 is the larger monthly scheme, with tickets priced at Rs 200. Its first prize is Rs 1.5 crore — the biggest routine first prize in the Punjab Dear family outside the quarterly and bumper draws. The second prize is Rs 10 lakh, with two prizes at that level, and the third prize is Rs 5 lakh, also with two prizes. Further tiers are paid on matching the last five digits in one tier and the last four digits in the remaining tiers, with several hundred prizes in the last tier.

Dear 200 suits players who want a much larger jackpot without waiting for a seasonal bumper. The trade-off is simple and worth stating plainly: the ticket costs four times a Dear 50 ticket, and the odds of winning the first prize are no better just because the prize is bigger. Buy it as entertainment with money you can afford to lose, never as an investment or a plan to recover losses.

## Punjab Dear 20: the low-cost monthly draw

At the other end of the price range sits Dear 20, a monthly draw held on the fourth Tuesday of the month, with tickets costing just Rs 20. The first prize is Rs 11 lakh, and the second tier carries ten full-number prizes. Lower tiers are paid on matching the last four digits, with several hundred prizes in the final tier.

Dear 20 is a good example of how Punjab's family is designed: the same draw mechanics, a much lower entry price, and a first prize that is still life-changing for most households. If you are new to Punjab lotteries, Dear 20 is the cheapest way to learn how the system works — how series, draw numbers and last-digit tiers behave — before you spend more on any other scheme.

## Punjab Dear 1000: the quarterly draw

Dear 1000 is the quarterly flagship of the family. It is drawn on the second Saturday of January, April, July and October, and each ticket costs Rs 1000. The first prize is Rs 50 lakh, the second prize is Rs 10 lakh, and the consolation prize is Rs 5 lakh. Below that, there are full-number prizes across several tiers, with hundreds of prizes in the final tier.

Because it runs only four times a year, Dear 1000 draws attract the most attention in the Punjab calendar after the bumpers. The same checking discipline applies, only more so: with a Rs 1000 ticket, double-check the draw date, the scheme name and the full ticket number before you conclude anything, and keep the physical ticket safe until the claim window closes.

## Punjab bumper draws: the seasonal jackpots

Alongside the regular schemes, Punjab runs seasonal bumper draws. The best-known recent example is the Dear Diwali Bumper 2026, drawn on 16 November 2026, with a first prize of Rs 12 crore and a ticket price of Rs 500. Bumper draws have their own ticket series, their own prize table and their own draw date, and they are not interchangeable with the daily or monthly schemes. A bumper ticket must be checked against that bumper's own result only.

Bumper prizes are also where scam calls cluster. If someone phones claiming you won and asks for a fee or bank details to release it, that call is a scam — genuine lotteries never ask winners to pay to collect. India's cybercrime helpline is 1930.

## How Punjab compares with other state lotteries

It helps to see Punjab in context. Nagaland's Dear draws run three times a day — 1 PM, 6 PM and 8 PM — with Rs 7 tickets and a first prize of Rs 1 crore. Kerala draws daily at 3 PM, with tickets costing Rs 40 to Rs 50 and a first prize of up to Rs 1 crore. Sikkim's Labhlaxmi draws daily at 4 PM, uses a plain 4-digit number, and pays a first prize of Rs 10,000. Punjab's family is different in structure: instead of one daily draw, it offers a ladder of daily, monthly and quarterly draws, so players can choose their price point and their prize level rather than taking a single format.

The rules that apply everywhere are the same in Punjab. Prizes above Rs 10,000 attract a flat 30% tax deducted at source (TDS). Winning tickets must be claimed within 30 days of the official result or gazette notification. Smaller prizes up to Rs 10,000 can generally be claimed through the seller, while larger prizes go through the state lottery directorate — in Punjab's case, the Directorate in Chandigarh — with identity proof and the original ticket.

## How to check your Punjab ticket, step by step

First, read your ticket carefully: scheme name (Dear 50, 100, 200, 20, 1000 or a named bumper), draw date, day name for Dear 50, series letters if any, and the full ticket number. Second, find the result for that exact scheme and date — not just any Punjab result from that day. Third, compare the full number for the first prize and the consolation prize, then check the last four digits (and last five, where the scheme uses them) for the lower tiers. Fourth, if anything matches, keep the ticket exactly as it is — do not laminate it, staple over the number, or hand the original to anyone except through the official claim process — and verify the match a second time against a second source.

One habit saves more disappointment than any other: check the date twice. Most wrong "I won" stories turn out to be a ticket checked against the wrong day's draw, especially with Dear 50, drawn every evening at 6:30 PM.

## A note on playing responsibly

Lotteries are a form of entertainment with very long odds, and no number, series, day or pattern changes those odds. There is no such thing as a hot number, a due number or a lucky series — every draw is independent. Set a fixed amount you are comfortable losing, stop when it is spent, and never borrow money to buy tickets or chase a loss with a bigger ticket. If playing stops feeling fun, stop playing.
