# How to Spot Lottery Scams: Fake Prize Calls, Advance Fees and What to Do Instead

## Introduction
Winning a lottery is exciting. Losing money to a fake lottery prize is not — and it happens far more often than most players realise. Every week, people across India receive a call, SMS or WhatsApp message telling them they have won a huge prize. It may use a real state lottery's name, a government-looking logo, or a photo of a real result sheet. But in almost every case, there is one catch: to claim the "prize", you must first pay a fee, a tax, or a processing charge.
That fee is the scam. A genuine lottery prize is never released only after you pay money upfront. This guide explains how these scams work, the red flags to watch for, and what to do if a scammer contacts you.

## Why Lottery Players Are Targeted
Scammers target lottery players for a simple reason: hope. If you regularly buy a ticket, a message saying "you won" sounds like the news you have been waiting for. Because results are public, scammers can name a real draw and a real-sounding prize amount to sound believable.
They also exploit urgency: almost every scam message creates a deadline — "claim within 24 hours or the prize will be cancelled." That pressure is deliberate. And they exploit trust in authority, pretending to be lottery, bank, tax or police officials.

## The Most Common Lottery Scam Patterns
### 1. The Fake Prize Call
This is the oldest and still the most common pattern. You receive a call from an unknown number. The caller congratulates you and says your mobile number, or a ticket number you supposedly hold, has won a prize — often several lakhs or even a crore. They may ask you to confirm your name, address and bank details "for verification".
What gives it away: they cannot tell you the exact draw name, draw number and ticket series that a genuine result would show. A real result is published openly; a scammer's prize exists only in their phone call.

### 2. The Advance Fee or "Tax" Demand
This is the heart of nearly every lottery scam. After announcing the prize, the caller asks for a payment before the money can be released. The reasons keep changing: GST, income tax, processing charges, courier charges, a security deposit, or a bank transfer fee. The amount is usually small compared with the promised prize — perhaps Rs 5,000 to unlock Rs 25 lakh — which makes it feel like a bargain.
The truth is simple: no genuine state lottery asks a winner to pay a fee in advance. Prizes above Rs 10,000 attract a flat 30% TDS, which is deducted from the prize itself — it is not collected separately beforehand. Once you pay the first fee, the demands rarely stop: a second charge follows, then a third. There is no prize at the end of that chain.

### 3. The Fake Result Sheet or Certificate
Some scammers send a doctored image: a result sheet with your number circled, a "winning certificate" with a government-style emblem, or a screenshot of a bank transfer supposedly on its way. These are easy to fake. The only reliable check is the published result for that draw. If your number is not on it, you have not won, no matter what image someone sends you.

### 4. WhatsApp, SMS and Telegram Variants
Not all scams arrive by phone call. Common message-based versions include:
- An SMS saying "Congratulations! Your number won the Dear Lottery" with a link or a phone number to call.
- A WhatsApp message from an unknown number, often with a lottery logo as the profile picture, asking you to forward the message or join a group.
- A Telegram or social media account promising "fixed numbers", "leaked results" or "guaranteed winning numbers" for a subscription fee.
No one can predict or fix a lottery draw. Anyone selling "sure numbers" is selling a fantasy — there is no insider list to buy.

### 5. The Fake Customer Care Number
Some scammers set up fake helpline numbers that appear in search results or social media comments. If a number does not come from an official source, treat it as unverified.

## Seven Red Flags That Mean "Scam"
Check every unexpected prize message against this list:
1. You did not buy a ticket for that draw. If you never bought a ticket, you cannot have won. Full stop.
2. You are asked to pay anything first. Fees, taxes, deposits, courier charges — any upfront payment is a red flag.
3. You are asked for bank details, OTPs or passwords. A genuine lottery office never asks for your OTP, UPI PIN, net-banking password or full card details over the phone.
4. There is extreme urgency. "Claim in two hours or lose it" is pressure, not procedure. Genuine claims are allowed within 30 days of the draw.
5. Payment goes to a personal account. Requests to pay into a personal bank account, a mobile wallet or through a gift card are hallmarks of fraud.
6. The caller cannot give verifiable details. They avoid naming the exact draw, draw number and series, or they get angry when you ask to verify.
7. The message came from an unknown number or unofficial page. Official results are published openly, not announced privately to individuals by SMS.
If even one flag is present, stop. Do not pay, and do not share personal details.

## What a Real Lottery Never Does
A genuine state lottery:
- Never contacts winners privately to announce a prize. Results are published publicly and winners come forward themselves.
- Never asks for an advance fee, tax payment or deposit before releasing a prize.
- Never asks for your OTP, PIN or password.
- Never announces results only through a private WhatsApp message or a phone call.
- Never sells "guaranteed" or "fixed" winning numbers.

## How to Check a Real Result Safely
If you hold a ticket and want to know whether you won, follow this safe routine:
1. Note your ticket's draw name, draw number, date and series from the ticket itself.
2. Check the published result for that exact draw and date — not a screenshot someone sent you.
3. Compare your full number carefully, digit by digit, including the series letter.
4. Use a ticket checker tool to double-check your number rather than relying on memory or a forwarded message.
5. If you genuinely match a prize, follow the official claim process for that state. Claims are normally accepted within 30 days of the draw, so there is no need to rush into a payment or a decision on the same day.
For reference, real draws include Nagaland Dear at 1 PM, 6 PM and 8 PM (Rs 7 ticket, first prize Rs 1 Crore), Kerala daily at 3 PM (Rs 40-50 ticket, first prize up to Rs 1 Crore), Sikkim Labhlaxmi at 4 PM (plain 4-digit, first prize Rs 10,000), Punjab Dear 50 at 6:30 PM (Rs 50 ticket), and Maharashtra weekly draws. Knowing the real schedule helps you spot a fake prize amount or timing.

## What to Do If a Scammer Contacts You
- Do not pay anything, and do not share OTPs, PINs or bank details. Hang up politely if you need to — you owe a stranger on the phone no explanation.
- Do not call back unknown numbers from prize messages, and do not click links in prize SMS messages.
- Take a screenshot of the message, note the number, then block it.
- Report it on cybercrime.gov.in or by calling the national cybercrime helpline 1930. Reporting quickly matters — banks can sometimes freeze a fraudulent transfer if alerted fast.
- Warn family members, especially elderly relatives, who scammers often target.
- If you already paid, act immediately: contact your bank, report the fraud, and keep all receipts and screenshots. Never pay a "recovery agent" promising a refund for another fee — that is usually a second scam.

## Protecting Yourself Going Forward
Buy tickets only from authorised sellers and keep your ticket safe. Never share photos of your ticket publicly before checking the result, and treat every unexpected prize announcement as a scam until the published result proves otherwise. Talk about scams openly with family — fraud thrives on silence.

## Frequently Asked Questions
Can a lottery office really call me if I win? No. State lotteries publish results publicly. Winners check the result and come forward. A private prize call is not how the system works.
But the caller knew my name and city. Does that make it real? No. Names, numbers and locations are widely available from data leaks and social media. Personal details in a call prove nothing about a prize.
I already sent money. Can I get it back? Sometimes, if the bank is alerted quickly. Contact your bank immediately, report the fraud on cybercrime.gov.in or by calling 1930, and keep every receipt and screenshot. Never pay a "recovery fee" to anyone promising a refund.
